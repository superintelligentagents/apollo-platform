# Apollo v2 — Participant Instructions

Apollo turns your real browsing into **long-horizon tasks**: things you actually worked on
across days or weeks — planning a trip, hunting for a job, researching a big purchase.

What happens to your work: your written request and checklist may join a research dataset that
is published or licensed for building and evaluating AI agents. If you build a task from Chrome
history, the selected visit-level history is also uploaded for internal validation, but it is not
included in a published or licensed dataset. That contribution is what you're compensated for.

**Privacy:** your history is read and grouped **on your device**. Nothing leaves your device until
you press *Submit task*, and only the journeys you selected are included.

## Getting started

1. Open the app (web link or the desktop app your study provided).
2. Sign in with your name and work email.
3. The bar at the top shows how many tasks you submitted. Click it any time to see your stats.
4. Unsure what a good task looks like? Tap **"See real examples"** on the home screen.

## Loading your history

Two of the three modes use your Chrome history, grouped on your device into **journeys**
(related browsing sessions). To load it:

- **Web, easiest — the history helper.** On the Load-history screen, download the small
  Chrome extension and load it once (chrome://extensions → Developer mode → Load unpacked →
  pick the unzipped folder). From then on the page reads your history in **one click** — no
  file to find, no quitting Chrome.
- **Web, no extension**: drop in your Chrome `History` file (quit Chrome first, or copy the
  file to your Desktop and drag in the copy).
- **Desktop app**: finds your Chrome profiles automatically.

Either way, nothing leaves your device until you submit a task. You must accept the data
contribution consent when you sign in; Apollo records its version and acceptance time with each
submission. For a history-backed task, the final screen lists how many selected journeys and visits
will upload. The upload contains their URLs, titles, visit times, search terms, and navigation links.
Raw history is used internally to validate the task and is never published, licensed, or sold.

## Three ways to make a task — pick whichever feels easiest

### 1. Write your own
No history needed. Pick **Blank page** (fill-in-the-blanks — replace the `[brackets]`, done)
or a **blueprint** (Plan a trip, Run a job hunt, …) and fill in one sentence per step: what
to compare, where, with what budget or dates. One filled step is enough; more steps make a
richer task.

### 2. Pick your journeys
Load your history, then search or filter your journeys, check the ones that belonged to one
project, and continue. **We draft the request from your own journeys** — replace the
`[brackets]` with the parts only you know, and submit. Even a single journey makes a valid
task.

### 3. Start from a theme
We scan your history for recurring threads — sites and topics you kept coming back to — and
show them as cards. Pick one, prune any journeys that don't belong, fix the drafted
`[brackets]`, and submit.

## What makes a task great

The best tasks read like a real request to an assistant:

> "I'm flying to a wedding in Palm Springs on the second Saturday next month. Find round-trip
> flights from Pittsburgh to LAX **and** to PSP, landing 2 days early — open the best of each
> so I can compare. Find a hotel near the venue under $200/night and rental cars at whichever
> airport wins; leave the choices ready for me to review without purchasing anything."

Three things do the work: **compare across options**, **real constraints** (dates, budgets,
places), and **what should exist at the end** (a plan, a booking, a shortlist).

### Make every task work later

Someone should still be able to do the task weeks or months from now. Use timing such as
**“next week”** or **“30 days from when the task starts.”** Do not include a fixed date, price,
schedule, availability, ranking, or answer that will go out of date. Ask the agent to find current
information when it does the task.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Couldn't parse that file"** (web): Chrome locks its History file while running — quit
  Chrome, or copy the file to your Desktop and drag in the copy.
- **No profiles found** (desktop): make sure Chrome has been used on this machine, then
  revisit the Load screen.
- **No themes found**: no multi-day threads stood out — use *Pick your journeys* or *Write
  your own* instead.
- **Closed the tab mid-task?** Reopen the app — it offers **Resume** with your writing intact.
- **Submit failed**: check your connection and press Submit again; nothing is lost.

Your submissions count immediately. Review happens separately and does not interrupt authoring.

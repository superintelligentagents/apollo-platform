Data construction and quality

High-quality tasks are made, screened, and only then selected.

The benchmark began with live, author-approved work from Turing’s reporting pipeline rather than a stale task snapshot. Quality control separated whether a task was fair and verifiable from whether an agent found it difficult.

01 / Construct

Annotator-built and signed off

Turing annotators wrote long-horizon requests and concrete rubrics, verified the intended websites and evidence, and approved the task before it entered the candidate pool.

Approved tasks only
02 / Screen

Fairness before difficulty

Starting candidates needed a genuine multi-step browser attempt, at least five rubrics, clean judge output, taxonomy coverage, and no content-filter exclusion. Twelve fairness-review agents then checked for stale values, access gates, underspecification, contradictions, and ungradeable evidence.

225 candidates → 211 fair
03 / Select

Representative by design

Rejected tasks could not return. Fair tasks were filled scarcest-category first, with category floors, an eight-task author cap, and subject spread; difficulty only broke ties within a category.

100 tasks · 20 categories · 22 authors
Quality-control correction

A 1,100-character export limit was found to truncate rubric text for 43 candidates. Every affected task was re-screened using the full rubric text, and those corrected verdicts replaced the truncated reviews.

Built on Odysseys

Turing annotators constructed long-horizon tasks that require agents to research and act across live websites, then reviewed and signed off on each task, rubric, and expected evidence. From the approved pool, we selected 100 tasks with genuine multi-step browser histories, no judge errors, and strong fairness, gradeability, category coverage, and author diversity; every model run is judged from its complete screenshot history.

What the trajectories reveal

Agents often gathered useful fragments but failed to convert them into the requested comparison, plan, spreadsheet, or other final artifact. Missing fields and weak source verification were visible too, but simple page-access failures account for a much smaller portion of the recorded judge explanations.

A step limit can mark a run that was already struggling, so the relationship between capped trajectories and failed rubrics is descriptive. It does not show that the cap caused the failure.
